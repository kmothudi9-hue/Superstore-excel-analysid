# Superstore Sales and Profitability Analysis

## Project overview
This independent learning project uses Microsoft Excel to analyse Sample Superstore sales and profitability. I explored performance across four regions, comparing product categories to identify strong performers, low margins and areas that may need further investigation.

I developed the project gradually while practising concepts from PwC’s Data Analysis and Presentation Skills specialisation.

## Business questions
- Which product categories generate the highest sales and total profit in each region?
- Which categories have the highest and lowest profit margins?
- Are there categories with strong sales but weak profitability?
- Where should the business investigate further?

## Tools and techniques
- Microsoft Excel for organising and analysing the data.
- PivotTables and filters to summarise and compare sales, profit and profit margin by region and category.
- SUM, AVERAGE and COUNTIFS, plus absolute cell references, practised during the project.
- Profit margin analysis, comparing total profit with total sales.
- Written commentary and business recommendations to communicate the findings.

## Key findings
- West: Furniture had the highest sales, but Office Supplies generated the highest total profit and profit margin. This shows that the highest-selling category is not always the most profitable.
- Central: Furniture recorded a loss and a negative profit margin, making it a priority for further investigation.
- East: Technology led the region in sales and total profit, while Office Supplies had the highest profit margin.
- South: Technology led in sales and total profit, while Office Supplies had the highest profit margin.

## Recommendations
Investigate low-margin and loss-making Furniture results at subcategory level. Examine discount patterns and other available factors, and compare them with stronger-performing categories. These patterns point to questions worth investigating; they do not establish the causes on their own.

## What I learned
This project helped me practise moving from spreadsheet calculations to business insights: organising data, summarising results, comparing performance and explaining what the findings could mean. It also reinforced the importance of distinguishing total profit from profit margin.

## How to explore
Download the Excel workbook and open it in Microsoft Excel. Explore the raw and cleaned data, analysis, financial analysis and insights worksheets.

## Limitations
This is a learning project using a sample dataset. Conclusions depend on the data and calculations available, and further investigation is needed before identifying the causes of weak performance.
